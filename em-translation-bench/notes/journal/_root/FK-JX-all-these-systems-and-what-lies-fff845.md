All these systems and what lies underneath?

I’ve recently focused on the way they all act as an attempt to teach you a way of thinking “clearly”, or to think without sin. 

What is sin in this concept I am presenting us here? For me, sin represents something which causes the self to feel the sensation of shame. If we choose to consume this fruit of shame, it will seed itself within us and grow as a self-thought, adding to our beliefs. Religions and spiritual movements seem to share in the way they seek to teach us how to redirect that shame energy and clear it out so that it does not grow a garden within you, and become the lens in which you behold your experience in the world. 

Sometimes, shame itself will lead into further actions which amplify the shame. This concept can be seen in the idea of the “deadly sin”. The shame becomes a root and the fruit of the sin. It grows and blossoms and in its blossoming, further shame occurs until the whole inner reality is one of dark dread- in this was our spirit feels dead, we are clamped down. 

Manifestation and new thought draws us into these ideas that our thoughts create our reality and even Jesus spoke of the way we can “move a mountain” quite literally “if we truly believe”. So we can imagine that when shame is the driving force within our psyches, how that would impact the reality around us. 

I want to take a moment to be clear here, because this part of “you create your own reality” is rife with harm and victim blaming potential. So many systems are double edged swords— they cut through to some underlying truth and yet also can be turned around as a weapon. So let us be clear, this reality is so tuned into the channel of shame on a global scale, that it is beyond just some easy idea of just “change your thoughts and happiness is yours”. 

For many of us, we have been brought up through generations of intolerance and abuse, words and actions and situations which preloaded within us a deep sense of shame. Shame for who we are. Shame for what we are not. Shame for what has happened to us. Shame for the things that did not happen for us. Shame for what we have and what we don’t have. For many of us, we have been born directly into this garden of shame, prebuilt by history, by our churches, by communities, by our own family’s hand, ofttimes. 

In this way, shame is the evil the Bible claims all humans received when we consumed the apple of knowledge in the garden. It is what we are all born with which keeps us away from the divine. It’s in through this lens I relate Adam’s first sin to the sensation of shame. It wasn’t that he ate the apple (or that Eve did), it was that through eating the fruit he was able to sense negativity, opposition, tension, and first of all beasts of emotion- shame. Both he and his body realize their nakedness, their temporary state, and panic. They hide from God, thus removing themselves from the good and the safety of the curated garden they were born within.

Shame acts as a polarity to freedom. Perhaps at some point we found ways to harness it as a teacher, claiming it’s sense was pointing to us that we have done wrong. We can see this in the telling of Eden where Adam and Eve are punished for disobeying and that the separation and all that’s happened. The experience he feels at the moment of eating the apple does nothing but “open his eyes” but with it came the shame and hiding from God and all those feelings of being a “naughty boy” who doesn’t deserve to be here. But let us suppose instead that in the beginning of our creation, we were in absolute connection with God. There wasn’t really any separation and this whole garden was filled with fruit worth eating and worth experiencing. 

Adamah is the Hebrew word for soil and represents the revealed Adam, what we can receive of him. This similar sense exists in Noah's name, where there is a revealed sense of him in Naha, a word with a sense of transport and guiding. This is why we are in the garden at this point in our symbolism because Adam is like the soil. He can take the idea of a seed and grow it into the full tree. So Adam can eat any of these fruits and via the consuming, he plants the seed within him and grows the tree. This bit can be likened to an experience: we grow from the seed the entire tree and then ourselves become capable of that fruit which we first experienced.
